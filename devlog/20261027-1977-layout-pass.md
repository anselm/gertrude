# 2026-10-27 · The 1977 layout pass

Anselm, mid-build: "Let's try to not use modern fonts typography and layout. What fonts were
popular in 1977? What color schemes? I am concerned that every site I see today looks the same."
Then: "Even layout style could be dense and 1970's - may require some research." A research agent
wrote a designer's brief with sources; this entry keeps what it found and what was applied.
Supersedes the "1977, first pass" section of `20261027-m1-talk-built-and-live.md`.

## What the research found (sources as given by the agent, read 2026-10-27)

**Type.** The decade's display work was the ITC catalogue: Souvenir (ITC's first release, 1971;
so overused it heads Garfield's "worst" list), Avant Garde Gothic (1970, from the magazine logo),
Serif Gothic (1972), Korinna and Tiffany (1974), Lubalin Graph (1974 or 1975, sources differ),
Bookman with swashes (1976, "an instant success"), Benguiat (1977, "defining typeface of the late
'70s and '80s"), Zapf Dingbats (1978). Cooper Black had its revival as the anti-Helvetica and was
"ubiquitous" by the decade's end; Windsor set the Annie Hall titles in 1977 on Benguiat's
suggestion. Helvetica ran corporate systems (Unimark, NYC subway map 1972, the National Park
Service Unigrid 1977). Bell Centennial (Carter, 1975–78) was drawn for phone books at 6 pt. Memos
and manuals were typed on Selectric elements: Courier, Prestige Elite, Letter Gothic. Phototype
let everything be set "tight not touching" and heavy. (en.wikipedia.org pages for each face;
fontsinuse.com/tags/1391/tight-not-touching; nps.gov/subjects/hfc/a-brief-history-of-the-unigrid.htm)

**Open-licence stand-ins.** Fraunces is the right display face: its README cites "Windsor,
Souvenir, and the Cooper Series" (github.com/undercasetype/Fraunces), with a SOFT axis and an
optical-size axis that makes it a Souvenir-ish text face at small sizes. Courier Prime (OFL) for
the typewriter; Cutive Mono is the truer Selectric Elite if wanted later. TeX Gyre Adventor
(Avant Garde) and Schola (Century Schoolbook) are free under the GUST licence. Nothing open
stands in for Korinna, Benguiat, Serif Gothic or Tiffany.

**Colour.** Harvest gold #DA9100, avocado #568203, burnt orange #CC5500, rust #B7410E,
chocolate #7B3F00, mustard #FFDB58 (Wikipedia infobox values; no paint-chip source turned up).
GE's avocado dates from 1966 and harvest gold 1967–68. Conventions: Apple's six-band rainbow
(1977), the NASA worm (1975), Southwest's Desert Gold stripes (1971), Braniff's single-colour
fuselages (1965). Rule: one cream ground, one dark ink, one or two spot colours, parallel stripes
of three to six bands as the only graphic.

**Dense layout.** The Whole Earth Catalog was set on an IBM Selectric Composer in narrow
justified columns with hairline rules, every entry boxed with price and supplier. Phone books:
6 pt, a heavy wide face for name and number, a light narrow one for the address. TV Guide was a
two-column log. Sears cut copy to fixed rectangles so no whitespace was left over. Newspaper
agate is 5.5 pt. Unigrid: black title band, rows and columns, Helvetica and Times. Screens: the
Apple II was 40 columns of uppercase; the VT100 (1978) 80×24 in white P4 or green P31 phosphor.
Distilled rules: hairline column rules instead of whitespace; 3–4 px rules under section heads and
a black band for the page title; square corners; all-caps letterspaced small heads and one big
soft display head per page; 14–15 px on 17–18 px leading, justified, first-line indents, no
paragraph space; dotted leaders; number everything; forms with fill-in lines, a "Form 1040" label
top left and a revision date top right.

The agent offered three concepts: (a) the typed transcript as a stack of index cards, (b) the
dense catalogue page, (c) the 80-column terminal. It recommended (a) for the chat and phones,
(b) for card, about and admin on wide screens, and the Unigrid band plus a four-stripe rule as
the shared signature. Agreed, and (c) is kept as a possible easter egg.

## What was applied

- **Masthead:** a chocolate title band with "Gertrude" in Fraunces 800 and a typed dateline, then
  an 8 px four-stripe rule (harvest gold, burnt orange, rust, chocolate). Inverts in dark mode.
- **Transcript as index cards:** each utterance is a card, cream a shade lighter than the page,
  1 px border, 3 px burnt-orange top rule, faint ruled lines every 24 px matching the Courier
  leading, speaker label in letterspaced caps, time stamp top right, "No. 3" bottom right.
- **Card page as Form G-1:** label top left, revision date and author top right, numbered fields
  with dotted leaders and a fill-in line for the name, numbered sections, two justified columns
  on wide screens with a hairline column rule.
- **About as a catalogue page:** display head, a typed dateline, three justified columns with
  first-line indents and numbered run-in heads, a colophon line.
- **Admin as a directory:** typed 12–13 px tables with hairline rules.
- **Everywhere:** square corners, hairline rules, typed uppercase buttons, a manicule (☞) for
  "see" and ✗ for errors, the menu as a numbered list in a hard-shadowed box, bottom-right on
  phones (thumb) and top-right on wide screens. Body text is Fraunces at optical size 12 and
  SOFT 60, which reads as the soft 1970s book serif the brief asked for without a third font.
- Not done: Cutive Mono, Adventor and Schola (no need shown yet); the terminal easter egg.
