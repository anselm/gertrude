# 2026-10-27 · Sousveillance, not the panopticon

Anselm, at the end of the day: "It's worth noting that Gertrude comes out of an idea of
sousveillance as opposed to the panopticon that most llm service providers emphasize. If we
share our conversations maybe we can get our needs met?"

Sousveillance is Steve Mann's word: watching from below, the recording done by the watched
rather than the watcher. The panopticon is the opposite arrangement, one centre seeing everyone.
Most hosted assistants are the second kind: the conversation goes up, the memory lives with the
provider, the person sees a summary at best. Gertrude's bet is the first kind: the person says
what they are willing to have known, sees exactly what she holds, and the sharing is the point,
because a need that nobody knows about cannot be met. This is the same instinct as his trust
work (attest: records public, reads private; "systems with no center") and the sous-essay
collection.

## What it already means in the design

- The card is the person's. They read it, correct it, delete everything, and see the quotes it
  rests on. The log is one record and it is theirs.
- Public by default, said so at first contact; private things are asked about and, if asked,
  not written down.
- Nothing about a third party is repeated; a name given to her stays a lead with a source, and
  the friend arrives by the friend's own hand.
- Nothing goes out unprompted without the person's channel consent and, for now, Anselm's yes;
  and what goes out is written into the log the person can read.
- Open source, so the watching apparatus itself is inspectable, and communities can run their
  own.

## What it asks for next

- **Reciprocity made visible.** If sharing is how needs get met, people should see what sharing
  bought them: the intros, the nudges, the outcomes, on their own card.
- **Opt-in public cards.** A person who wants their needs and offers findable should be able to
  publish their card, the way an ask is public on matchmaker; the default stays unpublished.
- **The graph seen from below.** Each person sees their own edges and the in-kind shape of the
  circle; the operator's admin page should not be the only view of the whole.
- **A line on the About page** saying this plainly. Draft, for his say, since it is his idea
  under his name: "She comes from an idea of sousveillance rather than surveillance: you decide
  what is known about you, you can see all of it, and sharing it is how your needs get met."
